Symptoms

How a Slab Leak Shows Up Before You See Water

Water under a slab has nowhere obvious to go, so the evidence is indirect.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking and heating the concrete above it.

Meter moves with taps off

Water is leaving the pipes somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

Sound of running water

A pressurized line is open when no fixture is on.

Damp carpet or lifted tile

Water is pushing up through the slab or along a crack.

Musty smell near baseboards

Moisture is moving through the slab into the house.

Low water pressure

A large leak can drop pressure before it ever surfaces.

Local Context

Why Slabs Hide So Many Leaks in South Florida

Many South Florida houses are built on concrete slabs, with supply lines laid in the sand below and drain lines cast in or under the concrete. Hollywood grew after its 1925 founding, and houses from the 1950s through the 1970s often still have original copper supply pipe beneath their floors.

Copper can develop pinhole leaks with age, and a line that rubs against rock or a rough slab edge wears through over decades. Soil moisture, a high water table and shifting ground add to the strain.

The result is a leak that can run for months without a puddle. That is why the first clue is usually the bill or the meter.

What You Will Notice

Floors, Dust and Matching Finishes

Opening a slab means cutting through whatever lies above it. Tile, wood and carpet all need removal in the work area. Matching an older tile or a discontinued flooring can be the hardest part of the job.

Ask about dust control, how the opening will be sealed and how the finish will be restored. If you keep spare tile from the original installation, give it to the crew. A plan that includes the finish up front avoids a second argument after the plumbing is done.

Paperwork

Insurance and Documentation for a Slab Leak

Repairs to supply piping are pressure tested as the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), requires for plumbing work. Under Florida Statute 627.70132, a property insurance claim is generally barred unless you notify the insurer within one year of the date of loss. Photograph the damage, keep the leak detection report and save every receipt.

If water has already soaked floors or walls, 24/7 water damage restoration starts drying quickly, and the moisture readings it records support the claim. For leaks on the buried line before the house, water line repair and replacement covers the outside run.

Honest Limits

What Limits a Slab Leak Test

Electronic listening works best on a pressurized line in a quiet house, so the test usually starts with every fixture off and the main valve open. Concrete thickness, tile and pipe depth change what the gear hears, and a very small leak can be quieter than the background noise.

That is why listening is paired with a pressure test of each zone and a look at the meter. When two methods point at the same spot, the breakout is small. When they disagree, we say so before anything is cut and explain whether rerouting the line is the better value.

Before Anyone Cuts

Questions to Ask Before Anyone Breaks Concrete

  • 1Which method located the leak, and what did the second method show?
  • 2How large will the opening be, and where exactly will it be?
  • 3What do spot repair, reroute and repipe cost, side by side?
  • 4Who handles tile, flooring or baseboards afterward?
  • 5What permit and inspection steps apply, and who schedules them?

Closing Up

After the Repair: Pressure Test, Photos and Warranty

Before the floor is closed, the repaired section should hold pressure for a stated period. Ask for photos of the exposed pipe, the repair and the finished patch, plus the pressure reading, and keep them with your home records.

Ask what the written warranty covers and for how long, and whether it covers only the repaired section or the new line. Those answers decide who pays if the same pipe fails again.

Until The Test

What to Do Until the Slab Leak Is Located

  • 1Turn the main valve off overnight and when you leave the house, then turn it on when you are home.
  • 2Read the meter morning and evening for two days and write the numbers down.
  • 3Move furniture and rugs off any floor spot that feels warm or damp.
  • 4Avoid running the hot water if a warm floor spot suggests the hot line is leaking.
  • 5Photograph any cracks, damp baseboards or stains with the date.
