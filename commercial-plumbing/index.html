Landlords and Tenants

Sorting Out Who Pays Before the Plumber Arrives

Commercial leases vary. A common pattern puts the building's main lines and risers on the landlord and the fixtures, drains and equipment inside the space on the tenant, but many leases differ. Check yours, and call the landlord or manager early.

Property managers handling several buildings should send one clear authorization and a billing contact. That avoids delays while the plumber waits for approval, and it keeps the invoice moving.

Food Service

Grease, Drains and Health Inspections

Kitchens send grease and solids into drains all day. Without regular cleaning, a line can plug in the middle of a dinner rush. Florida's rules require food service operations to control fats, oils and grease, and cleaning records are often checked by regulators.

A scheduled cleaning costs far less than a closed dining room, and a camera check after the first visit shows how fast grease rebuilds. Industrial parks in Pembroke Park and storefronts along the coast all share the same discipline.

Paperwork

Records a Property Manager Should Keep

Keep one folder, paper or shared, with backflow test reports, grease interceptor service receipts, water heater install dates and a marked map of shutoff valves. Inspectors and insurers ask for these, and a new manager inherits them.

Add the building's plumbing emergency contact and the water meter number. In a flood at night, nobody wants to search for either.

Leak Surveys

Commercial Leak Surveys: Parking Lots, Plazas and Multi-Unit Properties

Commercial leak calls look different from home calls. Water appears in an apartment complex parking lot, a shopping plaza keeps getting an alert from the water department, or a resort wants a broader survey instead of a single spot check. The first job is to decide whether the water is a supply line, a drain or irrigation.

We start with the meters and the shutoffs, then work section by section, closing off one building or one branch at a time. That shows which part of the property is losing water without digging the whole lot.

Fire mains and sprinkler lines are a separate category with their own rules and contractors. If the leak is on a fire line, we say so early and help you sort out who should handle it.
