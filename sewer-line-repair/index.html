Warning Signs

Signs the Problem Is the Sewer Line

One clogged sink is a drain problem. These point to the sewer line:

  • ✓Several fixtures back up together
  • ✓The lowest shower or floor drain fills when a toilet flushes
  • ✓Gurgling sounds from toilets or tubs
  • ✓Repeated clogs after professional cleaning
  • ✓A sewage smell near a cleanout or in the yard
  • ✓Wet or unusually green patches over the line

Diagnosis

Why a Camera Comes Before Any Digging

A sewer camera is a waterproof lens on a flexible rod that travels through the pipe and sends video to a screen. It shows roots, a crack, a joint that has shifted, a low spot where water pools and the point where a collapsed section begins.

Cameras also locate depth and position with a transmitter, so a repair opening is sized to the problem and not to a guess. You see the footage on site, and that evidence is what lets you compare a spot repair with a replacement fairly.

A recognized industry standard, NASSCO's Pipeline Assessment Certification Program (PACP), defines codes for pipe defects such as cracks, roots and offset joints. Ask whether the report uses those codes, since they make quotes from different contractors comparable.

Emergency Sewer Line

Emergency Sewer Line Repair: When a Mainline Clog Keeps Coming Back

A mainline clog that was cleared last month and is back this week is rarely just a clog. Roots, a sagging section or a cracked pipe hold the next blockage in place, and the backups tend to arrive at the worst time.

Treat it as an emergency when sewage comes up in a tub or shower, when water rises from a floor drain, when toilets gurgle in several rooms or when you smell sewage in the yard above a wet patch. Stop running water, keep people and pets out of the wet area and call. A camera shows the cause before anyone digs.

Tell the dispatcher where the lowest drain is, how many fixtures are affected and whether you have been through this before. If you know where your cleanout is, say so. That history tells the crew whether a cable machine is enough or whether the camera comes first.

Local Detail

Older Pipe and Rising Rates in Hollywood

Many Hollywood houses built in the decades after 1925 still carry original cast iron or clay drains. Cast iron rusts inward and cracks at joints, and clay opens at the hubs, so a 60-year-old lateral is a common find.

The city is also planning a long sewer program. With 44 percent of Hollywood on septic, the city's 25-year septic-to-sewer project is scheduled to start construction in 2032, so most of those properties will not see a new main for years.

If your property is mid-conversion or has a septic abandonment pending, tell the dispatcher. The repair plan and who pays can change.

Access

Cleanouts: The Cheapest Piece of Sewer Insurance

A cleanout is a capped access pipe on the lateral, usually near the house wall or at the property line. With one, a plumber can clear the line or run a camera without pulling a toilet, which saves time on an emergency. Many older Hollywood houses have none, or have one buried under sod.

If the lateral has no accessible cleanout, adding one during a repair costs little next to a second emergency call. Keep the cap clear of mulch and landscaping so the crew can find it fast at night.

Buying Or Selling

A Sewer Camera Check Before You Buy or Sell

A camera inspection of the lateral before closing costs a fraction of a replacement, and it shows pipe material, joint condition and any belly. In Hollywood, where many houses date from the 1950s and 1960s, it is the most useful single check on the underground plumbing.

Ask for the video and a short written summary. If the lateral is cast iron or clay with visible damage, you can negotiate the price or a repair credit with real evidence in hand.

After The Repair

After the Repair: Verify, Document and Protect the Line

Ask for a second camera pass after the repair so you can see the new section and the joints on each side of it. Keep the video with your home records, because the next buyer, insurer or plumber will want to see it.

Roots follow water, so avoid planting large trees or hedges directly over the lateral. If the old pipe is still clay or cast iron elsewhere, an annual look with a camera or a scheduled cleaning keeps a small root problem from becoming a second repair.
