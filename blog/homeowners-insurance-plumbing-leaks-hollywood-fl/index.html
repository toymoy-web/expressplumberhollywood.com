Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Plumbing Leaks in Hollywood, FL?

By the Hollywood Express Plumbing team · Published October 3, 2026

Florida homeowners policies typically cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, but often exclude slow or long-term leaks, flood from rising water and the repair of the failed pipe itself. Florida law also bars most property claims unless you notify the insurer within one year of the loss.

    What Is Usually Covered and What Is Not

    Policies differ, so read yours. This is the common pattern.

    SituationOften covered?Why
    Pipe bursts suddenly and floods a roomUsually yes for the damageSudden and accidental discharge
    Repair of the burst pipe itselfOften noWear and tear is typically excluded
    Slow leak over monthsOften noContinuous seepage is commonly excluded
    Tear-out to reach a hidden leakSometimesSome policies pay for access
    Flood from rising waterNo, needs flood policyExcluded from homeowners policies
    Sewer backupOnly with an endorsementCommonly excluded unless added

    The Florida Deadline You Cannot Miss

    According to Florida Statute 627.70132, a property insurance claim or reopened claim is barred unless you give the insurer notice within one year after the date of loss. A supplemental claim has an 18-month limit.

    The clock starts on the date of loss, not when you discover the damage. Report early and in writing, and keep a copy of how and when you reported.

    What to Document Before You Clean Up

    • 1Photograph and video every wet surface, the failed pipe or fixture and any damaged contents.
    • 2Keep the failed part if you can, since the adjuster may want to see it.
    • 3Save the plumber's report and receipts, including leak detection findings.
    • 4Take meter readings and keep recent water bills.
    • 5Write down the date, time and what you did to limit the damage.

    Limiting Damage Is Part of the Policy

    Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Shutting off the water, moving belongings and starting drying are expected. Delays that let mold spread can reduce a claim.

    Residential water damage restoration starts extraction and drying, and records moisture readings that help the claim. A leak detection report is useful evidence of where the water started.

    Miramar homes range from 1950s houses to new builds, and policies, deductibles and ages vary widely. Our Miramar page covers local housing details.

    The Exclusions That Surprise People

    Three phrases cause most disputes. Wear and tear means a pipe that fails from age is the owner's problem, even if the water damage it caused may be covered. Continuous or repeated seepage means a leak that ran for weeks or months can be excluded entirely. Some policies also cap mold coverage at a small amount.

    That is why finding a leak fast matters. A report that shows when the leak began, and a fix made as soon as it was found, supports the argument that the loss was sudden and that you acted quickly.

    Working With the Adjuster

    The adjuster inspects the damage and writes an estimate. Be present for the inspection if you can, and walk the adjuster through every wet area. Hand over the photos, the plumber's report and the receipts for drying.

    Ask for a copy of the adjuster's report and estimate in writing. If the estimate looks low, a restoration company or contractor can provide a second estimate for comparison. Keep a log of every call, with the date, the name and what was said.

    If a Claim Is Denied or Underpaid

    You have options. Ask the insurer to explain the denial in writing and to cite the policy language. Review the policy for the appraisal clause, which can settle disagreements about the amount of the loss.

    Florida's Department of Financial Services also runs a mediation program for residential property insurance disputes, and the Department's consumer helpline can explain how to use it. Time limits apply, so do not wait. An attorney who handles property insurance claims can advise on your rights.

    Reduce the Odds of a Denied Claim

    • ✓Replace washing machine hoses and old supply lines before they fail.
    • ✓Fix drips and running toilets promptly and keep the receipts.
    • ✓Know where your main shutoff is and use it if you travel.
    • ✓Keep the water heater and its pan in good condition.
    • ✓Read your policy once a year, especially after a renewal.

    How to Read Your Own Policy in Fifteen Minutes

    Find the declarations page first. It lists your coverages and deductibles, including any separate deductible for water damage or hurricane. Then find the section on perils, and read the wording around water, discharge, seepage and mold.

    Next, locate the endorsements. These add or remove coverage, and they are where sewer backup, water damage limits and service line coverage appear. Note the dollar limits beside each.

    Last, find the claim procedures. They state how and when to notify the insurer, what you must do to protect the property and what documents to send. Match them against the Florida one-year notice rule and use the stricter of the two.

    If anything is unclear, call your agent and ask for answers in writing. A two-line email now is worth more than an argument later.

    Five Questions for Your Agent

    QuestionWhy it matters
    Do I have water backup coverage?Sewer and drain backups are often excluded
    What is my water damage deductible?Some policies use a separate or percentage deductible
    Does the policy pay for tear-out to reach a leak?Access costs can be a large part of a hidden leak
    Is there a mold limit?Mold coverage is often capped
    Do I have service line coverage?Buried water or sewer lines are often excluded

    Small Leaks, Big Paperwork

    A small claim can raise a renewal premium, so weigh a modest loss against the deductible and the likely effect on your policy. Still, report a loss you are unsure about rather than letting the one-year window pass.

    Keep a folder with the policy, the photos, the plumber's report and every receipt. A complete folder makes the claim faster and puts you in a stronger position if the estimate comes in low.

    Keep the Evidence Simple

    One folder, one set of dated photos and one plain timeline beat a pile of scattered messages. Add the plumber's written cause and the date the repair finished. That folder answers most adjuster questions before they are asked.

    Florida Claim Deadlines for Hollywood Homeowners

    Under s. 627.70132, a property insurance claim or reopened claim is barred unless notice reaches the insurer within 1 year after the date of loss. A supplemental claim is barred unless notice is given within 18 months after the date of loss.

    The Florida Chief Financial Officer's guide to homeowners claims says the insurer should acknowledge a claim within seven days of your report. It also tells owners to make emergency repairs that protect the property from further damage.

    Owners in Miramar and Pembroke Pines follow the same state deadlines, and a dated plumber's report helps support the timeline.

    Sources: Fla. Stat. 627.70132, Florida CFO claims guide.
