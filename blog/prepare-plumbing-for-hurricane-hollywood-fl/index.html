Prepare Your Plumbing Before a Hurricane in Hollywood, FL

By the Hollywood Express Plumbing team · Published October 3, 2026

Before a hurricane, locate and test your main water shutoff, clear exterior drains, secure the water heater area and decide what to do about sewer backflow. Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30, so a quick plumbing checklist belongs on your preparation list every year.

    The Pre-Storm Plumbing Checklist

    • 1Find the main water shutoff and test that it closes fully.
    • 2Clear leaves from yard drains, gutters and downspouts so water can leave the property.
    • 3Check that the cleanout cap is on tightly and the area around it is clear.
    • 4Make sure the water heater's relief valve and drain pan are not blocked.
    • 5Take photos of fixtures, appliances and the meter reading for your records.
    • 6Keep the plumber's number and your insurance information in a waterproof bag.

    What Storms Do to Plumbing

    South Florida storms bring heavy rain, power loss and flooding. Rain raises the water table, which stresses septic drain fields. Power loss shuts off well pumps and lift stations. Flooding can overwhelm sewer systems, and floodwater can push into low drains.

    In November 2020, Tropical Storm Eta sent more than twice the normal flow to the regional plant that serves Hollywood, according to city officials. A system under that strain can back up into the lowest fixtures in a house.

    For Septic Owners

    About 44 percent of Hollywood relies on septic. If your tank is due for pumping, do it before the wet season peaks, and stay off the drain field. After a storm, ground that is still saturated may not accept more wastewater, so limit water use until it drains. Septic pumping is the simplest prevention.

    After the Storm

    Do not use tap water until the utility says it is safe if a boil-water notice is in effect. Avoid floodwater, which may contain sewage. If a drain is backing up, stop using water and call.

    A camera check of the lateral after a major storm finds cracks that let stormwater in, and emergency sewer line repair addresses them. If water has soaked floors or walls, emergency water damage restoration handles drying before mold starts.

    Generators, Gas and the Water Heater

    Never run a generator indoors or in a garage. Carbon monoxide kills quickly. Keep generators well away from doors and windows and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

    If you evacuate, switch the water heater to off or vacation mode and close the gas valve to the heater. If power returns while you are gone, a heater that comes on with a leaking tank can do damage.

    When you return, check the area around the heater before restoring power or gas.

    Water Safety After the Storm

    Utilities may issue a boil-water notice after a storm if pressure drops or a main breaks. Follow it exactly, and use bottled or boiled water for drinking, brushing teeth and food prep until the utility lifts it.

    Flush toilets only if the sewer is working, and run each tap for a minute to clear the lines when service returns. If the water looks cloudy or smells odd, wait and check with the utility.

    A 72-Hour Plumbing Timeline

    • ✓72 hours before: test the main valve, clear drains and photograph fixtures.
    • ✓48 hours before: pump the septic tank if it is overdue and fill containers with drinking water.
    • ✓24 hours before: secure the water heater area and decide whether to close the main.
    • ✓During the storm: avoid using water if the sewer or tank may be overloaded.
    • ✓After: check for leaks, wet ceilings and backups, and call for help early.

    Condos and High-Rises

    Condo owners should ask management what the building does before a storm: whether risers are shut off, how elevators and pumps are handled and who holds keys to units. A leak in an empty unit can flood the floors below before anyone notices.

    Leave management a contact number and the name of a local person who can enter your unit. Taking photos of the unit before you leave helps if damage occurs.

    Build a Plumbing Record Before You Need One

    Walk through the house with a phone and record each room, with a close look at the water heater, under sinks and the laundry area. Narrate the age of fixtures and any recent repairs.

    Store the video and your plumber's contact in the cloud and in a waterproof bag. If a storm damages the house, that record supports the claim and shows what was working beforehand.

    Add the meter reading and the location of the main valve to the same note.

    Renters and Landlords

    Renters should ask the landlord where the main valve is and who to call for plumbing damage. A tenant who moves belongings off the floor and reports damage quickly protects the deposit and the claim.

    Landlords should confirm sump or drain function, check that exterior drains are clear and keep a contact list of plumbers and restoration companies handy.

    Do It Before the Cone Appears

    Plumbing preparation works best in May, not when a storm is on the forecast. Test valves, clear drains, pump overdue tanks and record the house. When the cone appears, you will only have to close a valve and make a call.

    Hurricane Season Dates, Evacuation Orders and Flooded Septic Systems

    The National Hurricane Center says the Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. The Florida Division of Emergency Management says your local emergency management program should always be consulted regarding evacuation orders.

    After flooding, EPA says not to use a septic system until water in the soil absorption field is lower than the water level around the house. The Florida Department of Health fact sheet says to minimize water use and not to repair the tank and drainfield until the flood water has receded.

    Those agencies differ on pumping, so ask the county health department before you schedule it. Owners in Dania Beach and Hallandale Beach near the water should plan their shutoff and sewer steps now.

    Sources: NOAA National Hurricane Center, Florida Division of Emergency Management, EPA septic systems after a flood, Florida DOH flood and septic fact sheet.

    Insurance Reminders

    Flood from rising water needs a separate policy, and flood coverage typically has a waiting period before it takes effect, so do not wait for a storm in the forecast. Florida law generally requires notice of a property claim within one year of the loss.
