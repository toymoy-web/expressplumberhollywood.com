Start With Facts

What Is Actually in Your Tap Water

Before buying a filter, read the city's annual water quality report. It lists the disinfectant, the regulated contaminants and test results. Hollywood's Water Quality Division can answer questions about your address at 954.921.3414.

Hollywood draws water from the Biscayne Aquifer, treats it with lime and ferric chloride at the plant and filters it. Regional reports describe chloramine as the disinfectant. Taste and odor complaints often trace to the disinfectant, and sediment complaints often trace to old pipes inside the house.

Start With The Symptom

What You Notice and What It Usually Means

Chlorine or swimming pool smell

Disinfectant taste and odor. Carbon or catalytic carbon addresses this.

Cloudy or sandy water

Sediment from the supply or old pipes. A sediment filter helps.

Rust-colored water

Corroding pipe inside the house. A filter is a stopgap and repiping is the fix.

White crust on fixtures

Hardness scale. A softener, not a filter, addresses it.

Bad taste from the kitchen tap only

A point-of-use carbon or reverse osmosis system suits this.

Water that looks or smells different overnight

Check the utility for notices before buying anything.

Certification

How to Read Filter Claims

  • ✓NSF/ANSI 42 covers taste, odor and chlorine reduction.
  • ✓NSF/ANSI 53 covers health-related contaminants such as lead and certain chemicals.
  • ✓NSF/ANSI 58 covers reverse osmosis drinking water systems.
  • ✓A filter is certified only for the claims listed on its data sheet.
  • ✓Ask for the data sheet, not just the box.

Honest Limits

What a Filter Cannot Do

A filter does not make unsafe water safe unless it is specifically certified for that purpose, and we do not make health claims about individual exposure. If you have a health concern, talk to your doctor.

A filter will not replace maintenance. A neglected carbon cartridge can grow bacteria, and a clogged sediment filter will cut your pressure. Plan to replace cartridges on schedule.

If Something Changes

What to Do If Your Tap Water Suddenly Looks or Smells Different

  • 1Stop drinking and cooking with the water until you know the cause.
  • 2Run the cold tap for a few minutes and check whether the change affects every tap.
  • 3Check the Hollywood Public Utilities website or call its Water Quality Division for notices.
  • 4Do not rely on a household filter to treat water under a boil-water notice.
  • 5Call a plumber if only some taps are affected, which points to a house problem.

Local Notes

Old Pipes, Hard Water and What Filters Cannot Fix

Rust-colored water often comes from corroding galvanized pipe inside the house, and a filter is a short-term answer to a pipe problem. If you see rust or flaking, faucet and sink repair addresses fixtures, and a repipe addresses the cause.

Condo owners should confirm whether a whole-house system is allowed, and whether the main is accessible. Hallandale Beach buildings share risers, so under-sink systems are often the practical choice there. See the Hallandale Beach page for building rules.

Water Filtration Questions

Public water systems must meet federal and state standards and publish annual reports. The City of Hollywood's Water Quality Division at 954.921.3414 can share its latest report. A filter changes taste, odor and some contaminants, but it is a choice and not a substitute for the utility's standards.

Water utilities add disinfectants to keep water safe in the pipes. Regional reports describe chloramine as the disinfectant in this area, and some people notice its taste or smell. A catalytic carbon filter is the usual way to reduce it.

NSF/ANSI 42 covers aesthetic effects such as taste and odor, 53 covers health-related contaminants and 58 covers reverse osmosis systems. Ask which standard and which claims a filter has been tested for.

A whole-house filter treats every tap and protects appliances from sediment. An under-sink system treats drinking and cooking water more thoroughly at one point. Many homes use both.

Cartridge life depends on the water and the product. Sediment filters and carbon cartridges are commonly changed every several months to a year, and reverse osmosis membranes last longer. Follow the manufacturer's schedule.
