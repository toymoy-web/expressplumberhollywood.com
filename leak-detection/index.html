Start With the Symptom

What a Hidden Leak Looks Like Before You See Water

Most callers do not know where the water is going. They only know something changed. Match what you notice to the likely source:

Meter spins with taps off

A leak somewhere between the meter and a fixture. The main-valve test tells us which side.

Bill jumped, usage did not

A slow leak, a running toilet or an irrigation fault. Often cheaper to fix than the bills suggest.

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab, heating the concrete above it.

Sound of running water at night

A pressurized line is open somewhere, often inside a wall.

Musty smell, swollen baseboards

Moisture trapped in a wall or cabinet base.

Low pressure at every tap

A service line leak can drop pressure before it ever surfaces.

Why Speed Matters Here

Water Gets More Expensive Every Year in Hollywood

Hollywood's commission approved a five-year rate plan in July 2026. According to the city's Public Utilities Department figures reported by Axios, the typical water-only bill rises from about $33 a month now to more than $68 by 2031, and the combined water and sewer bill from roughly $99 to $223.

The EPA estimates the average household's leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons a year, and a leaking toilet alone can lose around 200 gallons a day. One rate calculator built on the city's fiscal 2026 rates puts each extra 1,000 gallons at about $15.59 on the combined bill. At that rate, a 200-gallon daily toilet leak adds roughly $90 a month, and the sewer share rises with every future increase.

A leak that costs $90 this year costs much more in 2031, which makes the diagnostic visit one of the cheaper decisions on this page.

Emergency Leaks

Emergency Leak Detection: When a Hidden Leak Cannot Wait

Most leak calls can wait for a daytime visit, but some cannot. If the meter keeps spinning with every tap closed and you are losing about a gallon a minute, water is leaving the pipes right now. Close the main valve at the house, read the meter again and call.

Treat these as emergencies too: water coming through a ceiling or out of a wall, a wet patch that spreads across the yard, a utility alert saying water has been flowing nonstop, and a leak found during an inspection with a closing date close. Each one gets worse every day, and the repair is smaller when the pipe is found early.

Tell the dispatcher what the meter did, where you see water and whether the building is a home, a rental or a property for sale. A landlord with several units or a seller with a deadline needs a clear written finding as much as the repair. The line is open 24/7.

Housing Stock

Older Homes, Older Pipe Materials

Hollywood grew after the city was founded in 1925, and many homes from the 1950s through the 1970s were built on concrete slabs with supply lines laid below them. Copper supply pipe can develop pinhole leaks with age, and older galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and restricts flow before it leaks.

That matters when a leak is found. Repairing one pinhole in a house full of aging pipe often buys months, not years, and an honest report tells you whether the surrounding pipe looks sound or whether a repipe conversation belongs on the table.

Salt air along Hollywood Beach and the Intracoastal adds corrosion to exposed valves and fittings, so leaks in beachside condos often start at a connection and not mid-pipe.

Paperwork

What a Leak Detection Report Should Contain

A useful report names the method that found the leak and the pipe that failed. It should place the leak in plain terms, such as the wall behind the hall bath or the line between the meter and the house, with photos of the marked spot and the meter reading you both saw. Without those details, an insurer or an association has little to act on.

It should also keep three costs apart: finding the leak, repairing the pipe and restoring finishes. Ask for the repair estimate in writing before any wall is opened, and ask what the opening will look like when the work is done. A report that stops at leak found leaves you to start over.

Outside The Walls

Leaks Outside the House: Service Line, Irrigation and Pool Lines

Not every hidden leak sits inside a wall. The buried line from the meter to the house, a sprinkler zone and a pool supply line can all lose water into the ground, and in sandy South Florida soil the water often drains away without leaving a puddle.

The meter test separates the two cases. Close the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If it stops, the leak is inside the house, and if it keeps moving, the leak is on the buried side, which is where water line repair and replacement and pool leak detection come in.

Around Hollywood

Leak Detection From Hollywood Beach to Emerald Hills

Oceanfront Hollywood Beach and the blocks near the Broadwalk are full of condos and hotels where a leak can come from a unit above. Inland neighborhoods such as Hollywood Lakes, Hillcrest and Emerald Hills are mostly houses, where the meter and the yard lines come first.

That split decides the plan. In a condo the first stop is the shared riser, and in a house it is the meter and the slab.

Under the Floor

Under the House, in a Crawl Space or Under a Mobile Home

Not every building sits on a slab. Some have a crawl space or a raised floor, and mobile homes have an enclosed underbelly, so a leak can run for weeks before water shows. Clues include a musty smell, soft flooring, a damp underside and a meter that moves with every tap closed.

We check the meter and the pressure first and then look where the lines run, through an access panel or a crawl space opening where one exists. In a mobile home the skirting panels and belly insulation are the first places to open.

If another plumber has already tried and missed it, tell us. The earlier tests save time, and the next step is often a different method, such as isolating one line at a time, instead of repeating the same check.

Rentals and Sales

Rentals, Multi-Unit Buildings and Homes for Sale

A landlord with a duplex, triplex or four-unit building often cannot tell which unit the water is coming from. Tests that isolate one unit or one line at a time narrow it down before anyone opens a wall.

Sellers and buyers meet a different version. An inspection finds a stain or a high reading, the closing date is near, and the question is whether the problem is a small repair or a large one. A written finding with the location and your options gives everyone the same facts.

Property managers ask for the same details each time: where the leak was, how it was confirmed and what repair is suggested. Tell us at the start if you need a report for an owner, a tenant matter or a closing.
