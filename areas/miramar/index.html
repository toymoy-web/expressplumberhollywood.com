Growth

From Fewer Than 200 People to More Than 120,000

Miramar incorporated on May 26, 1955, with fewer than 200 residents and about 2.9 square miles bounded by SW 64th Avenue, University Drive, Pembroke Road and the Miami-Dade line. Its first mayor named the city, and Miramar means look at the sea.

Today the city covers more than 30 square miles and counts well over 120,000 residents. That pace of building means a single street can hold a 1960s house, and the next development over holds a house less than 20 years old.

The plumbing lesson is simple. Ask when the house was built before assuming what is behind the wall.

Large Homes

Slab Leaks in Big Homes: Quiet, Expensive and Worth an Emergency Call

Many Miramar homes sit on concrete slabs with long supply runs to second-floor baths and outdoor kitchens. A small leak under the slab can run for months before a bill or a warm floor spot gives it away.

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and a water heater replacement in a large home has to meet it. Because the house is large, the first test is the meter. If it moves with everything off, electronic leak detection splits the house into zones and tests each. For a tankless unit that has stopped producing hot water, emergency water heater repair covers diagnosis, descaling and part replacement.

Yards and Irrigation

Irrigation, Pools and Backflow in Miramar Communities

Newer Miramar communities carry extensive irrigation. Florida's building code has required a backflow preventer on residential irrigation since 1990, and many Broward cities, Hollywood included, require annual testing. Check your own city's rule.

Pool equipment lines and irrigation lines also leak without much sign. A wet patch near the equipment pad or a sprinkler zone that stays soggy is worth a test before it becomes a bill problem.
