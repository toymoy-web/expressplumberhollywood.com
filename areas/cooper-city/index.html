The City

An 8-Square-Mile City With Parks at Its Center

Cooper City was incorporated on June 20, 1959, and counted 34,401 residents in 2020. The city covers about 8.3 square miles, of which roughly 0.3 is water.

It is bounded by Davie to the north, Pembroke Pines to the south and Southwest Ranches to the west, and both Hollywood and Davie lie to the east. Parks give the city its identity, including Brian Piccolo Park, Bill Lips Park, the Cooper City Sports Complex and Flamingo West Park.

For a plumber, a residential city of this size means one dominant house type, the single-family home on a lot with a garage, a yard and an irrigation system. The common failures follow from that.

Emergency dispatch for Cooper City runs around the clock, so a tank that fails at night gets the same attention as one that fails on a quiet morning.

Yards and Pools

Irrigation, Pool Lines and Backflow in Cooper City Yards

Many Cooper City lots carry irrigation zones and pool equipment, and the buried lines age quietly. A soggy patch that stays wet between rains, or a sprinkler zone with weak pressure, deserves a test before it becomes a bill problem.

Florida's building code has required a backflow preventer on residential irrigation since 1990, and Broward cities commonly require annual testing. Check your own city's rule, and keep the report on file.

Who Fixes What

Separating a Utility Break From Your Own Pipe

A break at the street, low pressure across the neighborhood or a sewer main backing up is a utility matter. A leak after your meter or a clog in your own lateral is yours.

Check your water bill for the name of the utility that serves your address. If you are unsure which side the water is on, close your main valve and watch the meter. A meter that stops means the problem is inside your property.

Common Calls

Water Heaters, Repipes and Hidden Leaks Lead the Tickets

The most common emergency in a typical home here is a tank water heater that fails in the garage. Residential water heater repair starts with a repair-or-replace answer, and a leaking tank body always means replacement.

The second pattern is a pipe that keeps pinholing. Repairs follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and when the pipe is at the end of its life a whole-home repipe is the permanent fix.
