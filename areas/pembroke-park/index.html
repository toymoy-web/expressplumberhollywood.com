The Town

A 1.6-Square-Mile Town With 17 Mobile Home Parks

Pembroke Park incorporated on December 10, 1957, and takes its name from Pembroke Road. The land was once nurseries, rock quarries and mobile home parks, and the quarries are now lakes that cover about six-tenths of a square mile of the town's 1.6.

The town counts 17 mobile home parks, four of them for recreational vehicles, and about half of residents live in mobile homes. Along I-95, modern warehouses fill the industrial areas.

So Pembroke Park plumbing is really two kinds of plumbing: mobile home systems with exposed lines under the home, and commercial buildings with floor drains and backflow devices.

Emergency dispatch for Pembroke Park runs around the clock, which matters when a supply line fails under a home at night. Say whether the property is a mobile home, a house or a warehouse, and the dispatcher plans the visit around it.

Along I-95

Emergency Plumbing for Warehouses and Small Businesses

The town's industrial side hosts warehouses and offices. These buildings run floor drains, break rooms and restroom lines on schedules that are easy to forget until something backs up.

A commercial property in Pembroke Park should test its backflow assemblies annually and keep the report on file, and repairs must meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). For slow floor drains, cable cleaning and camera inspection come first, and high-pressure hydro jetting handles repeat buildup.

Ground Conditions

Former Quarry Lakes and a High Water Table

Quarry excavation left lakes across the town, and the ground sits low and close to the water table. A lot that stays soggy after the rainy season may be groundwater and not a leak.

If a yard stays wet after the rains end, or a lot connection weeps near the ground, a short test tells a leak from a groundwater problem. Where a pit pump handles the groundwater, sump pump repair keeps it working.
