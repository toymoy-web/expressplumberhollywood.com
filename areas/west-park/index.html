The City

A 2.26-Square-Mile City Built From Four Communities

West Park became Broward County's 31st city on March 1, 2005, after residents voted roughly 3,400 to 956 to incorporate. It was assembled from four communities that had been unincorporated, Carver Ranches, Lake Forest, Miami Gardens and Utopia.

The city covers 2.26 square miles and counted 15,130 residents in 2020. It sits directly south of Hollywood, with Pembroke Park on the east side and Miramar on the west and Miami-Dade County just below.

A city that small has a short list of main roads. Pembroke Road, SW 56th Avenue, Hallandale Beach Boulevard and Miramar Parkway cross or touch it, and I-95 runs alongside, which is why a crew from Hollywood reaches most addresses without a long detour.

Emergency dispatch for West Park runs around the clock, so a failed water heater at 3 a.m. gets the same answer as one at noon. The dispatcher asks for your cross street first and the year the house was built second.

City or You

Sorting Out a Utility Problem From a Property Problem

Low pressure across the block, a sunken patch in the street or a manhole bubbling over is a utility matter. A wet spot in your own yard, a running meter or a clog that only affects your house is a property matter.

Your water bill names the utility that serves you, and the account number on it is what the utility asks for first. If you are unsure which side of the meter the leak is on, close your main valve and watch the meter. A meter that stops means the problem is inside your property.

Where We Start

Water Heaters and Hidden Leaks Lead the Tickets

A tank water heater that rusts through is the most common emergency in a typical house here. Tank water heater repair begins with a plain repair-or-replace answer, because a leaking tank body cannot be patched.

The next pattern is a meter that moves with every tap closed. That points to a slab or yard leak, and hidden leak testing finds it before anyone breaks concrete. Repairs follow the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
