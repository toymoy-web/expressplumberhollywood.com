Water and Money

A Running Toilet Is Quietly Expensive

The EPA says a leaking toilet can waste about 200 gallons a day. At a combined Hollywood water and sewer rate of roughly $15.59 per extra 1,000 gallons, according to one rate calculator, that is close to $90 a month.

Rates are rising through 2031 under the city's plan, so the same leak costs more each year. A fifteen-dollar flapper beats a bill like that every time.

If you are unsure whether a toilet is the source of a rising bill, the dye test works. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank without flushing, wait fifteen minutes and look for color in the bowl.

Emergency Toilets

Emergency Toilet Repair: Overflowing, Leaking and Backing Up

A toilet that overflows, leaks at the base or runs water out of the tank can do real damage in minutes. Turn the small valve behind the bowl clockwise to stop the supply. If the bowl keeps rising, lift the tank lid and press the flapper down to stop the flow.

If the toilet will not flush and water also rises in a tub or shower, the blockage is further down the line, and it becomes an emergency sewer backup call. Do not keep flushing.

Call if the shutoff valve will not close, if water reaches the ceiling below or if a leak at the base will not stop. A broken flush lever or a leak in a one-bathroom home, or in a home with an older resident, is a same-night problem. Say so when you call.

Replacement

When a New Toilet Pays for Itself

Older toilets use far more water. The EPA says WaterSense-labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, while older models can use 3.5 gallons or more. A household that flushes thirty times a day saves real money on water and sewer charges with a modern model.

Replacement is also the answer for a cracked bowl, a failing glaze or a toilet that has needed three repairs in two years. Taller comfort-height models and elongated bowls are options, and the rough-in distance decides what fits.

Often Overlooked

Check the Flange Before You Blame the Toilet

  • ✓The flange should sit level and firmly secured to the floor.
  • ✓A cracked or rusted flange lets the toilet rock and leak.
  • ✓Floor height changes after new tile can leave the flange too low.
  • ✓A soft floor around the toilet means water has been leaking for a while.
  • ✓Fixing the flange and subfloor first prevents a repeat leak.
