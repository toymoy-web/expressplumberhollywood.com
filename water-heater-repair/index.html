Decision Framework

Repair or Replace: Four Questions That Settle It

How old is the unit? Most tank heaters last roughly ten to fifteen years, and repairs on a unit near that range often come due again within a season.

Where does it leak? A leak at a fitting is a repair. A leak from the tank body is a replacement every time.

What would the repair cost against a new unit? If the repair is a large share of replacement, the new warranty and efficiency usually win.

Is the tank rusting or scaled inside? Heavy corrosion means the next failure is not far off.

Hollywood's water comes from the Biscayne Aquifer, a limestone and sand formation, and the city treats it with lime to reduce hardness. Treated water can still deposit scale on elements and the tank floor, so a yearly flush is worth the half hour it takes.

Maintenance

A Yearly Checklist That Extends Heater Life

  • 1Flush a few gallons from the drain valve to clear sediment.
  • 2Lift the relief valve lever briefly and confirm water flows and stops.
  • 3Have the anode rod checked around year three, then every couple of years.
  • 4Look for rust, drips or moisture at fittings and in the drain pan.
  • 5Set the thermostat near 120 degrees.
  • 6Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes and chemicals.

Two Kinds Of Heater

Gas and Electric Heaters Fail in Different Ways

A gas heater depends on the burner, the flame sensor, the gas valve and the vent. Failures show up as a pilot that will not stay lit, a yellow flame or a smell of exhaust, and the vent has to be checked before the unit is relit.

An electric heater depends on two heating elements and two thermostats. Water that runs lukewarm and then cold often points to a failed element, and a meter test finds it quickly. Both types share the same tank problems, which are sediment, a spent anode rod and a leaking tank.

If your unit is tankless, tankless water heater service covers descaling and error codes.

Safety Valve

What Water at the Relief Valve Is Telling You

A relief valve opens when pressure or temperature climbs too high. A steady drip can mean excess supply pressure, a failed expansion tank or a valve that has worn out, and a gush means the heater needs to be shut down.

The valve is a safety device, so it should never be capped or plugged. If it drips, note when it happens, such as after a hot shower or all day, because that pattern points to the cause.

Capacity

When the Heater Is Too Small and Hot Water Runs Out

Sometimes the heater works and the household has simply outgrown it. A tank that was sized for two people runs out quickly with four, and the usual signs are hot water that fades halfway through the second shower and then recovers slowly.

Check the label for the tank size and the first-hour rating, which is how much hot water the unit delivers in its first hour. If the rating is well below what your household uses at peak, repair will not fix the shortage, and a larger tank or a different type is the real answer.

Small Savings

Five Habits That Cut Heating Cost and Failures

  • 1Insulate the first few feet of hot pipe near the heater.
  • 2Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees, which also lowers scale buildup.
  • 3Install or keep a drain pan with a line to a safe drain.
  • 4Turn the heater to vacation mode, or off, if you will be away for weeks.
  • 5Replace the anode rod before the tank wall starts to rust.
