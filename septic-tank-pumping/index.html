Local Context

Why So Many Hollywood Homes Run on Septic

The City of Hollywood's Public Utilities Department says 44 percent of the city still relies on septic tanks. About a third of the septic tanks left in all of Broward County sit inside Hollywood city limits.

Sun Sentinel reporting in 2019 put most of those properties west of Federal Highway, and the city has since adopted a rate plan to fund a 25-year project that replaces the tanks with central sewer, starting in 2032. If you live in that western half of the city, assume your house is on septic until the utility confirms a sewer connection in writing.

That timeline matters for one practical reason. A tank that is not pumped on schedule will not last until the sewer arrives, and a failed drain field costs far more than regular pumping.

Warning Signs

Signs the Tank Is Full or the Field Is Failing

Two or more of these together mean call and say septic first:

  • ✓Every drain in the house runs slowly, not just one sink
  • ✓Gurgling from toilets or tubs when the washing machine drains
  • ✓A sewage smell near the tank lid or over the drain field
  • ✓Soggy ground or unusually green grass above the field after dry weather
  • ✓Wastewater backing up into the lowest shower or floor drain
  • ✓A tank that has gone more than five years without pumping

  3. 3

  4. 4

    Report

    You get a plain-language result and a pumping interval.

The inspection is the part that saves money. A cracked baffle or a tank that refills within days points to a field problem, and you hear that before you decide on repairs. If the trouble is actually the house line, sewer line repair work starts with a camera and not with a second pumping.

Wet Season

Why Backups Spike in Hollywood's Rainy Months

South Florida's water table sits close to the surface, and it rises through the summer rains. A drain field needs unsaturated soil to treat wastewater, so heavy rain slows it down even when the tank itself is fine.

That is why a system that behaved all winter can back up in August. Cutting water use for a few days during a storm, spreading laundry across the week and keeping vehicles off the drain field all give the soil time to recover.

If the backup stays after the ground drains, the field has likely lost capacity and needs evaluation. Do not run chemical cleaners through the system, because they kill the bacteria that break down waste in the tank.

Planning Ahead

Keep a Septic File Before the City's Sewer Project Arrives

The city's 25-year plan connects septic homes to sewer in phases starting in 2032, so your records may matter more than you expect. A folder with pumping dates, inspection reports and repair receipts shows the system was maintained and helps whoever plans your connection.

Add a sketch of the tank and field with the lid location measured from two fixed points on the house. Lids get buried under sod, and a sketch saves a technician a search on the day of a backup.

After Pumping: Keeping the System Healthy

A pumped tank starts a new cycle, so the next three to five years depend on habits. Spread laundry over several days, fix dripping fixtures that add steady flow and keep trees with aggressive roots away from the field.

If the problem turns out to be a clog between the house and the tank, we move to drain cleaning and unclogging. The same steps apply in neighboring Pembroke Pines and Miramar.
