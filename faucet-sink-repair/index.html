Local Water

How South Florida Water Wears Out Faucets

Hollywood's water is drawn from the Biscayne Aquifer, a limestone formation. The city treats it with lime and ferric chloride to reduce hardness and color, but dissolved minerals can still leave scale on aerators, cartridges and showerheads.

Scale shortens the life of a faucet and clogs its screens. Cleaning aerators every few months and replacing cartridges when they start to drip keeps a fixture working longer.

Salt air near the beach corrodes finishes, especially on outdoor fixtures and hose bibs.

Under The Sink

What to Do Under a Leaking Sink

  • 1Dry the cabinet floor and run water to see where the drip appears first.
  • 2Look at both supply line connections and the shutoff valves.
  • 3Check the P-trap joints, which can loosen over time. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), calls for a shutoff valve at each fixture, so know where yours are.
  • 4Look at the disposal connections if there is one, since a leak there can mimic a faucet leak.

Why It Matters Here

Humidity Turns a Small Leak Into Mold

Cabinet bases are particle board, and South Florida humidity keeps them damp. A slow drip under a sink can swell the panel and grow mold within weeks, long before a stain appears on the floor.

If you see a swollen cabinet base or smell mustiness, check for a leak. When the source is unclear, leak detection testing separates a fixture leak from a wall or slab leak. Disposal connections can also leak, covered under garbage disposal repair and installation.

Replacing It

Choosing a Replacement Faucet That Will Last

Start with the number of holes in the sink or counter, because that sets which faucets fit. Then look at the cartridge, since ceramic disc cartridges resist wear better in mineral-heavy water, and at the finish, which should be rated for daily wiping.

WaterSense-labeled bathroom faucets flow at no more than 1.5 gallons per minute, which cuts water use without much change at the sink. Ask the installer to reuse your shutoff valves only if they are sound, and to replace them if they are stiff.
